Topic 1

Cell Types

All living things are made of cells. There are two fundamental types — knowing the difference is essential for every biology exam.

Eukaryotic
Has a true nucleus enclosed in a membrane. Includes animal, plant and algal cells. Size: 10–100 µm.
Prokaryotic
DNA floats freely in cytoplasm — no membrane-bound nucleus. All bacteria are prokaryotic. Size: 1–5 µm.
Both have
Cell membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes. That's it — everything else differs.

Topic 2

Microscopes & Magnification

You need to be able to use the magnification formula, work in standard form, and compare the two types of microscope.

Light microscope
  • magnification up to ×2,000
  • resolution ~200 nm
  • specimens living or dead
  • cost cheap
  • image colour
  • see cells, nucleus, chloroplasts
Electron microscope
  • magnification up to ×2,000,000
  • resolution ~0.1 nm
  • specimens dead only
  • cost very expensive
  • image black & white (false colour added)
  • see ribosomes, membranes, viruses
Resolution = ability to distinguish two separate points. Higher resolution = finer detail. Electron microscopes can resolve ribosomes (~20 nm); light microscopes cannot (limited to ~200 nm).

Topic 3

Cell Division & Specialisation

Cells divide by mitosis to produce identical daughter cells. Specialised cells are adapted for specific functions.

Chromosomes
Humans have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs) in body cells. Each chromosome is a long DNA molecule carrying hundreds of genes.
Genes
A gene is a section of DNA that codes for a specific protein. Different base sequences → different proteins.
Mitosis result
Produces two genetically identical diploid daughter cells. Used for growth, repair, and asexual reproduction.

Differentiation — process by which a cell becomes specialised for its function.

In animals: mostly during early development. Mature cells largely lose the ability to differentiate.

In plants: cells can differentiate throughout life. Meristem cells remain undifferentiated permanently.
Exam tip — where does mitosis occur?

In animals: bone marrow, skin, gut lining, embryo.

In plants: meristems at root tips and shoot tips.

Mitosis does NOT occur in mature neurons or red blood cells.
Topic 4

Stem Cells & Cancer

Stem cells are undifferentiated cells with the ability to become specialised. Cancer results from uncontrolled cell division.

Animal stem cells
  • Embryonic: pluripotent — can become almost any cell type
  • Adult (e.g. bone marrow): multipotent — limited range, mainly blood cells
  • Used in therapeutic cloning — patient's DNA used to grow replacement tissues
Plant stem cells (meristems)
  • Found at root tips and shoot tips
  • Totipotent — can become any plant cell type
  • Used to clone plants via tissue culture on nutrient agar
  • Meristems remain undifferentiated throughout the plant's life
Benefits of stem cell research
  • Potential to treat currently incurable diseases (paralysis, diabetes)
  • Therapeutic cloning reduces organ rejection risk
  • Could grow new β-cells to treat type 1 diabetes
  • Could repair spinal cord damage (paralysis)
Risks & ethical issues
  • Embryo destruction raises ethical and religious concerns
  • Stem cells could develop into tumours
  • Risk of infection from transplanted cells
  • Treatments not always reliable or consistent
Cancer — benign vs malignant
Benign tumour ✓ (less dangerous)
  • Grows slowly
  • Stays in one place
  • Does not invade other tissues
  • Usually not life-threatening
  • Can be removed by surgery
Malignant tumour ✗ (dangerous)
  • Grows faster
  • Spreads (metastasis) via blood/lymph
  • Invades other tissues
  • Life-threatening
  • Called cancer when malignant
